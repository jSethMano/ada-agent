## Summary

Use this guide when an email appears fraudulent, unexpectedly requests sensitive information, contains a suspicious attachment, or directs you to an unfamiliar sign-in page. Warning signs include mismatched sender details, urgent demands, and requests for passwords or verification codes.

## Steps

1. Avoid clicking links, opening attachments, replying, or scanning QR codes included in a suspicious email.
2. Check the sender's full email address and review the message for unusual requests, unexpected payment instructions, or suspicious links.
3. If the email claims to come from a colleague or business contact, verify the request using a known, trusted communication method rather than replying to the suspicious message.
4. If your approved email application provides a phishing-reporting option, use it according to company guidance.
5. Keep the message available for investigation unless company reporting instructions say otherwise. Do not forward it to coworkers or distribute potentially malicious attachments.

## If that didn't work

- Ask the IT helpdesk how to report the message securely if the approved reporting function is unavailable.
- If you clicked a link, opened an attachment, entered credentials, or approved an unexpected authentication request, report that action promptly.
- Preserve relevant message details for investigation without sharing sensitive information unnecessarily.

Do not attempt to investigate a suspicious file yourself or hide a potential security incident.

## When to file a ticket

Report the message immediately when it appears suspicious, even if you have not interacted with it. Escalate urgently if you disclosed credentials, downloaded a file, or approved an unexpected sign-in request. Include the message details and actions taken, but never include your password.
